# Provider commercial authorization transition

Tracking: `niu-gh3`. This document specifies the replacement contract; it does not assert that the transition has been implemented.

Connecting a Provider with an API key and discovering its models is an integration task. It must not require a commercial review or represent a credential as a successful upstream verification. Internal, owner-funded use remains separate from platform resale.

For resale, an authorized platform administrator must explicitly enable an offer for its current model and agreed purchase-rate revision. The enable action is the commercial authorization: it must identify the exact Provider, offer and revision, record the responsible actor, and create an immutable authorization record. Publishing a rate, importing models or saving a credential alone must not enable an offer. An offer also requires a matching enabled route and credential; a workspace needs its own selling price, authorization and sufficient funds. None of these records proves live upstream availability.

## Lifecycle and compatibility

- Enabling must fail closed if the current revision or route changed since the administrator reviewed it. Lock and recheck the Provider, offer and route within the existing serialized route-write transaction; never authorize a caller-supplied stale revision. Media offers additionally retain their exact model, schema, route and ownership binding and existing liability qualification requirements.
- A rate publication, incompatible route or credential change, revocation, or other existing invalidation must pause the affected offer and invalidate its current authorization. Re-enabling requires an authorized action for the new current revision. Pausing and provider-wide disabling must have clear consequences; a Provider member must not gain platform procurement authority through this change.
- A prepared attempt must pin the authorization current when it was admitted. Before dispatch, it must still match the offer's current authorization and revision and satisfy the existing route, media binding and financial checks. A concurrently invalidated authorization fails closed. Historical attempts, rates, charges and settlements retain their original references and meaning.
- Preserve historical hash-review and revocation records without altering their meaning or inventing evidence. Existing active offers must retain their prior eligibility and expiry semantics until an explicit transition is made. A new authorization must be distinguishable from a historical evidence review, be immutable and auditable, and must not be represented by fabricated digests or a fabricated expiration. Compatibility APIs must not report the new authorization as an independently reviewed evidence record.
- Existing foreign keys, dispatch triggers, registry publication, invalidation triggers, and settlement references must be migrated together. An additive schema transition alone is insufficient; all reads, writes and consumers must consistently distinguish the old and new records. Do not substitute an unconditional qualification predicate or remove `niu_media_offer_binding_current`.

## Verification requirements

Exercise the complete migration from an existing database containing active, expired and revoked legacy reviews and historical attempts. Test installation and platform-administrator authorization, denied ordinary users and Provider members, stale revisions, repeated enable and pause, credential and model changes, and concurrent invalidation between admission and dispatch. Verify text and media admission separately, including media liability bounds and customer balance isolation. Confirm that imported routes without an explicitly enabled offer are not exposed as sellable inventory, and that no customer-facing response reveals procurement rates. Verify key-only onboarding, discovery failure and recovery, and truthful availability in internal and resale modes in the rendered application.
